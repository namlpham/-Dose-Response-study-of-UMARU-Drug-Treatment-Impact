# Dose Response study of UMARU Drug Treatment Impact
Description: Drug addiction is a major public health concern that affects individuals, families, and communities. While drug treatment programs have been established to help patients recover from addiction, the effectiveness of these programs varies significantly. The UMARU IMPACT study provides the "drugtreatment-1.csv" dataset, which includes information on factors that may impact the likelihood of staying drug-free, such as demographic factors, mental health scores, and drug-use history. The dataset comprises 575 observations, with randomization occurring at two sites that used different treatment modalities. Site A randomized 444 individuals to test the difference between 3- and 6-months treatment, while site B randomized 184 individuals to receive either 6- or 12-month treatment. The purpose of this research report is to investigate the predictors of staying drug free, with a specific focus on the duration of a drug treatment program.

Summary of analysis results: 
Based on the dataset provided by the UMARU IMPACT study, we found that patients with a history of recent IV drug use, a higher number of prior drug treatments, and younger age should be targeted for special drug treatment design to increase their chances of remaining drug-free. Additionally, we found that longer treatment durations appear to be more effective in helping patients remain drug-free in this particular study. However, it is important to note that the imbalanced classification problem inherent in the dataset was not addressed during our analysis, which may have affected the accuracy and validity of our model. Therefore, we recommend further discussion and improvement of the dataset or data collection design of the study before drawing any definitive conclusions about the predictors of remaining drug-free.
